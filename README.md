# TXTM8 Keyboard

An Android keyboard for the old and more efficient way of typing on a phone: a number pad with T9-style predictive text.

Press each number once for the letter you want. The keyboard works out the word, learns which words you use, and gets better at putting them first.

You can also switch to multi-tap, where repeated presses cycle through the letters, or use the full QWERTY keyboard when you need it.

The predictive engine starts with a 40,000-word English dictionary, ranked by frequency. It also learns from your typing, including which words tend to follow each other.

There are more than 60 themes to choose from, including Nokia phones, game consoles, old operating systems and 90s anime, plus a Custom mix theme where you build your own from the parts of the others. On top of that there is a range of typing, editing and layout settings.

Everything runs locally. There is no internet permission, and anything the keyboard learns stays on your phone.

## What it does

### Predictive typing

Press each number once for each letter. For example, `4663` gives you suggestions for GOOD, HOME, GONE and other words with the same key sequence.

The best guess appears in the text as you type. Other suggestions appear in the strip above the keys. Press `*` to cycle through them, or tap a different suggestion directly.

Press space (`0`) to accept the highlighted word.

### Multi-tap

Hold `#` to switch between predictive and multi-tap modes. The label under `#` shows which one you are in.

Multi-tap works like older mobile phones: press a number repeatedly to cycle through its letters. For example, `7` gives P, Q, R and S.

The letter is entered when you press another key or the timeout expires. The word you've typed so far appears in the suggestion strip, and tapping it adds it to your dictionary, so predictive mode will find it next time.

Hold a number key to enter the digit itself. Keep tapping it and you'll reach its letters. In number and phone fields, the number pad enters digits directly.

If you'd rather start in multi-tap, you can choose that in Settings.

### It learns from you

Words you choose more often gradually move up the suggestions list.

Type a new word using multi-tap and tap it in the strip, or type it on the QWERTY keyboard, and it will be added to the predictive dictionary for next time.

It also remembers which words tend to follow each other. After a space, the suggestion strip can offer likely next words based on what you've typed before.

You can remove a suggestion by holding it. Removed words stay out of the list, even after restarting the keyboard. Holding a next-word suggestion removes that word pairing instead.

Everything it learns is saved locally. You can clear learned words at any time from Settings.

### Full QWERTY keyboard

Tap the keyboard icon to switch to a conventional QWERTY layout, with a separate numbers and symbols page.

It includes shift, space, enter and backspace keys, along with clipboard controls and the same cursor and selection gestures as the number pad.

If you'd rather the keyboard always opened on QWERTY, switch on Default to QWERTY in Settings. You can also add a row of digits above the letters.

### Emoji and text smileys

The emoji page is available from either symbol page by tapping `;)`.

It contains emoji grouped into sections, followed by old-school text smileys and kaomoji. Each section scrolls sideways, with the whole page scrolling up and down.

### Editing gestures

- Slide along the space key (or `0` on the number pad) to move the cursor.
- With text selected, use the same gesture to adjust either end of the selection.
- Slide left from backspace to select text backwards. Let go to delete the selection.
- Flick quickly left across backspace to delete the whole previous word.
- Hold the clipboard key and let go to copy selected text.
- Hold the clipboard key and slide to select text from the cursor. Let go to leave the selection in place.
- Tap the clipboard key to paste.
- Backspace straight after a word re-opens it with its suggestions, so you can choose a different one. Tapping a word you've already typed does the same.

Backspace always removes one character at a time. Gestures can be switched off or adjusted for speed in Settings.

### Automatic typing features

- **Capitalisation:** Automatically capitalises the start of sentences. Tap `#` to cycle between lower case, a capital for the next letter, and caps lock. The letters on the keys change to show which one is next.
- **Capital I:** `I`, `I'm`, `I'll`, `I've` and `I'd` always get a capital I.
- **Contractions:** Fixes common missing apostrophes when typing letter by letter. `dont` becomes `don't`, `im` becomes `I'm`, and so on.
- **Double space:** Two quick taps on space after a word insert a full stop and a space.
- **Automatic spacing:** Accepting a suggestion adds a space after the word. Typing punctuation or a closing bracket straight afterwards takes that space back.
- **Punctuation:** Repeatedly tap `1` to cycle through punctuation marks. Hold `1` and keep holding to open the symbols page.

Each of these can be switched off in Settings.

### Themes and settings

There are more than 60 inspired themes to choose from, in sections:

- **Essentials:** Follow system, Dark and Light, plus **P5**, black and white with a choice of twelve accent colours, and **Custom mix**.
- **Phones and gadgets:** Nokia 3210, 3310, 3510i and 7210, Sony Ericsson T610, Razr V3, Kawaii Flip Phone, Amber Backlight, Pager, Palm Pilot and Walkman.
- **Game consoles:** Game Boy, Game Boy Advance, SNES (US and EU), Virtual Boy, N64, GameCube, Saturn, Dreamcast (US, Japan and EU), PlayStation, PS2, PSP, Xbox and N-Gage.
- **Video games:** WipEout 2097, WipEout HD, MonsterGear and MonsterNav.
- **Film and TV:** Saved by the Bell, Star Trek LCARS, Evangelion Unit-00, Unit-01 and Unit-02, Serial Experiments Lain, Ghost in the Shell, Hackers and Matrix.
- **Retro computers:** MS-DOS / BIOS, Terminal Green, Terminal Amber, Retro Desktop, Windows 95 and Windows XP.
- **Web and media players:** Netscape Navigator, GeoCities, Winamp and Media Player.
- **Twinkling stars:** Glitter Graphics, Night Sky, Winter, Aurora, Synthwave and Gold on Black. Pixel-edged keys with stars that flash at the corners.
- **Tap animations:** Bubbles, Embers, Sakura and Neon Ripples. Things appear wherever you tap, not just on the key.

Many of them have their own custom animations behind the keys or under your finger taps.

**Custom mix** lets you build your own theme. Take the colours, key style, background, animation and font from any theme, each one separately. There are a total of 74 colour combination palettes, 37 button styles, 397 possible background gradients, 34 animation packages, and 15 fonts to choose from, for a total of **7,491,390 total possible combinations**. 

Key size can be set to small, medium or large. The typing pulse, where the background flashes as you type, works on every theme and has an adjustable strength. The star themes have a star-density setting, and the tap-animation themes have an effect-amount setting.

On the main screen, Previous and Next step through the themes while the keyboard stays open, so you can try them in the test box.

The Settings page, opened with the cog at the top right, is split into sections:

- **Layout:** Default to QWERTY, a number row on QWERTY, and swapping Enter and Backspace, with a separate switch for the number pad, QWERTY and the emoji page.
- **Typing:** Next-word suggestions, a space after tapping a suggestion, capitalising sentences, double-space full stops, capital I, contractions, re-opening words, which mode to start in, and the multi-tap timeout.
- **Gestures:** Slide gestures on or off, and slide speed.
- **Feedback:** Vibration and key click sounds, each on or off.
- **Your words:** Switch learning on or off, or clear everything learned.
- **More:** The About page with a full feature guide, and Reset settings.

### Password and number fields

Password fields automatically switch to the plain QWERTY keyboard, with predictive typing and learning disabled.

Number and phone fields use the number pad to enter digits.

## Setup

1. Install the APK. Download it from the Releases page on GitHub. Android may ask you to allow installs from your browser or files app.
2. Open TXTM8 Keyboard.
3. Press **Enable the keyboard** and enable it in Android's keyboard settings.
4. Return to TXTM8 and press **Switch to TXTM8 Keyboard**.
5. Select TXTM8 Keyboard from the keyboard picker.

The main screen shows these steps first. Once both are done, the theme and test sections appear, and you can try the keyboard in the test field there.

## Requirements

- Android 8.0 (API 26) or later.
- An English-language dictionary is included.

<summary><b>How it works</b></summary>

### Predictive engine

The predictive engine uses a digit-keyed trie to find words matching the sequence entered on the number pad.

Each word is assigned a base score derived from its frequency in the source word list. More common words start higher in the suggestions.

Exact matches are shown first, followed by longer words that begin with the same key sequence.

The engine keeps a shortlist of likely completions at each point in the trie, rather than searching through the entire dictionary every time a key is pressed.

### Learning

Every time a dictionary word is chosen, its user score increases. This changes its ranking without changing the original dictionary.

The engine also records word pairs. If a word regularly follows another word, it receives an additional ranking bonus when that previous word is present.

Next-word suggestions use these learned pairs, ranked by how often they have occurred.

Words typed in multi-tap or QWERTY mode can be added to the dictionary, so they are available to predictive mode afterwards.

Removed suggestions and learned data are saved in a local file and restored when the engine starts again. Typing a removed word letter by letter adds it back.

### Dictionary

The bundled English dictionary contains 40,000 words.

It is derived from [FrequencyWords](https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords), using word-frequency data from the OpenSubtitles corpus.

The original frequencies are converted to a logarithmic score:

`score = max(1, round(100 × ln(count)))`

This keeps the scores within a useful range for the engine's own learning weights.

The dictionary is filtered to remove invalid words, fragments and unwanted apostrophe forms. Common contractions are included separately so they remain available.

The dictionary's source and licence information is in `dictionaries/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

The dictionary can be rebuilt using `tools/build_dictionary.py`.

### Key mapping

The number pad follows the traditional telephone layout:

| Key | Letters |
|---|---|
| 2 | ABC |
| 3 | DEF |
| 4 | GHI |
| 5 | JKL |
| 6 | MNO |
| 7 | PQRS |
| 8 | TUV |
| 9 | WXYZ |

Accented characters are normalised to their unaccented equivalents for lookup. Apostrophes are ignored when encoding words, so `don't` and `dont` use the same key sequence.

### Android integration

The keyboard is implemented as an Android Input Method Service.

The predictive engine is kept separate from the Android keyboard interface, allowing it to be tested independently.

The keyboard communicates with the active text field through Android's input connection. It handles composing text, committing words, cursor movement, selection, clipboard actions and editor-specific actions.

Password fields are detected through the editor's input type. Learning and predictive suggestions are disabled for these fields, and the keyboard switches to the plain QWERTY layout.

### Themes

Every theme is built from five parts: colours, keys, background, animation and font. The keyboard only ever reads the finished theme, never which one it is, which is what lets Custom mix take each part from a different theme.

Themes are built once and reused, so switching between them doesn't rebuild anything. If you want to add one, `docs/THEMES.md` explains how.

### Tests

The predictive engine has a Kotlin test suite covering:

- Number-to-letter encoding.
- Exact matches and word completions.
- Frequency-based ranking.
- Learning and user-added words.
- Previous-word suggestions.
- Saving and restoring learned data.
- Removing words and word pairs.
- Multi-tap session behaviour.
- Contraction handling.

## Building from source

The project uses Kotlin and Android Gradle Plugin.

Requirements:

- JDK 17
- Gradle 8.10.2
- Android SDK 34

Run the tests:

```bash
gradle :test
```

Build the debug APK:

```bash
gradle :app:assembleDebug
```

The APK is generated in:

```text
app/build/outputs/apk/debug/
```

## Known limitations and issues

This is an early version, and some things are missing or rough. Here is what to expect.

- **English only.** There is one English dictionary, and the contraction and capital-I fixes are English too. Other languages are not supported yet.
- **Words that aren't in the dictionary.** Names, slang and newer words may be missing. The dictionary comes from film and TV subtitles, so it also has a few odd entries. Add a missing word once with multi-tap or QWERTY, and hold a suggestion to remove one you don't want.
- **QWERTY is deliberately plain.** There is no typo correction and no accented letters. The number pad is where the prediction lives.
- **Learned words can't be backed up.** There is no export or restore, and clearing is all or nothing. Uninstalling the app loses everything it has learned.
- **The emoji page is simple.** There is no search, no recently used section and no skin tone options. How the emoji look depends on your phone's emoji font.
- **Changing a setting restarts the keyboard.** If it's open in the test box on the main screen, it closes and reopens so that it picks up the change.
- **Gestures can be triggered by accident.** The slide and flick gestures are on by default. If they get in the way, slow them down or switch them off in Settings.
- **Number, phone and password fields are fixed.** Number and phone fields only enter digits, and password fields always use QWERTY with learning switched off.
- **Installing the APK.** It is installed by hand, so there are no automatic updates. Builds aren't signed with a fixed key yet, so v0.6.5 won't install over a previous version. Uninstall the old one first, which also clears what it has learned.
- **Android's keyboard warning.** Android shows a generic warning when you enable any keyboard, saying it may be able to collect what you type. This keyboard has no internet permission, so it has no way to send anything off your phone.

If you find something that isn't listed here, please open an issue on GitHub.

## Privacy

There is no internet permission in the Android manifest.

The dictionary and predictive engine run locally. Learned words, word preferences, word pairings and removed suggestions are stored in the app's private storage.

Nothing is uploaded, and learned data can be cleared from the settings screen.
